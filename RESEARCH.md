# RESEARCH.md — what the v4 redesign is built on

Five research sweeps (2026-08-19) grounding the v4 UI/UX + engagement + pedagogy work. Full
transcripts died with the session; this is the distilled, load-bearing part: every applied design
move, and the anti-patterns we explicitly refused. Key citations inline where they matter.

The spine of it: dopamine fires on ANTICIPATION, not payoff (Schultz 1997), and peaks at 50/50
uncertainty (Fiorillo 2003) — so the bet is the unit of engagement. Retrieval with spacing is the
largest learning effect ever measured (g≈0.61 testing, g≈0.74 spaced; Adesope 2017, Latimier 2021).
Endowed progress makes people finish what already looks started (Nunes & Drèze 2006). Feeds are
literal Skinner boxes (Lindström 2021, Nature Comms) — DRIP aims the same machinery at retention
of understanding instead of retention of attention.

## The dopamine/engagement loop: what makes short-form feeds compulsive, and how to aim that 

applied:
- Timeline, framing: adopt the small-area rule. Early in a topic arc the hairline emphasizes distance travelled ('3 deep into memory allocation'); once past the arc midpoint it flips to remaining-to-payoff ('2 cards to the flex'). Never a global percent, never a fraction — this operationalizes Prime Directive #5 with Koo & Fishbach as the spec.
- Timeline, anatomy: render three states so 'how much is generated' is legible at a glance — solid segments behind the position marker (viewed, immutable), dimmed-but-present segments ahead (generated runway, 'more is ready'), and a fading tail that terminates in a crossroads glyph (unwritten future, ends in a choice not a void). This answers the owner's 'where am I / what's left / what exists' complaint in one visual grammar.
- Timeline, endowed start: pre-light the first 2-3 segments for real completed acts (paste ingested, clarify answered, hook viewed) so a fresh session visibly starts 'already in progress' — Nunes & Drèze's 34%-vs-19% completion lift, done honestly by crediting genuine actions.
- Structure every topic into micro-arcs of 5-8 cards ending in a visible checkpoint, and make the timeline show distance-to-NEXT-checkpoint, not session totals — goal-gradient acceleration only fires against a near, visible goal; many small finish lines beat one long bar.
- Make the swipe the lever pull: every payoff (predict reveal, reveal payoff, bet outcome, checkpoint flex) lands only AFTER a committed gesture — next slide or explicit tap — never passively on the same screen state. Keep strict one-card full-screen exclusivity and never add autoplay; learner pacing is a named moderator of the segmenting effect (g≈0.36).
- Add variable-ratio insight jackpots: extend lib/generation/variety.ts with a jackpot cadence — roughly 1-in-5 cards with jitter is a high-production 'aha' (a stat that reframes everything, a twist reveal, a one-two hook+payoff) at unpredictable positions, so the reader can never predict WHICH swipe pays off. The slot-machine schedule, but the coin is understanding.
- Calibrate bets for anticipation, not comfort: keep the ~80% baseline hit rate for competence-building, but schedule deliberate near-50/50 curveball bets at arc midpoints (learner-state already tracks calibration) — Fiorillo's sustained dopamine ramp peaks at p=0.5, and the pre-answer moment on a genuinely uncertain bet is the single most gripping beat the feed can produce.
- Reorder for the pretesting effect: place a predict/binary BEFORE the concept card that resolves it (guess-then-learn, not learn-then-quiz), and require revealCopy to restate the correct answer explicitly every time — errorful guessing + immediate feedback beats read-first, but unfed errors persist, so the feedback half is non-negotiable.
- Spaced callbacks disguised as callbacks: re-surface a concept from 15-40 cards back as a fresh bet styled in callback voice ('still think X?'), at expanding intervals within and across sessions — spaced retrieval is g≈0.74, the largest learning effect available, and the feed format hides the review completely (it reads as continuity, not homework).
- Hypercorrection lever: use tap latency on scored cards as a confidence proxy; a FAST wrong answer triggers the loudest reveal treatment (biggest spring, sharpest contrast, one extra beat of payoff copy) — confidently-wrong-then-corrected is the best-remembered event in the memory literature, so spend the juice budget exactly there.
- Session creation as the first dopamine hit: collapse paste-to-first-hook below ~3 seconds perceived by emitting hook #1 from the paste alone while the planner runs behind it, and render clarify questions AS feed cards (they already are) so setup is indistinguishable from the loop — the reader should be 'in' before they've decided to be. TikTok's own habituation number is 35 minutes total; the first 30 seconds decide whether there's a session at all.
- Interruption hygiene + honest open loops: system surfaces (notices, crossroads, refresh affordance) appear only at arc boundaries, never mid-concept-run (2.8s interruptions double error rates); and a skipped interactive card stays visibly UNCLOSED on the timeline — a small hollow segment — rather than counting as progress, so skimming past a bet leaves a salient gap that invites return instead of rewarding the skim.

refused (the anti-patterns):
- Never optimize the adaptive loop on dwell/session-length alone. That is TikTok's objective function, and TikTok's own unsealed documents concede compulsive use correlates with loss of analytical skills and memory formation. DRIP's reward signal must be bet hit-rate plus retention on spaced callbacks — engagement metrics are diagnostics, not the target.
- No leaderboards, no social-comparison badges, no percentile rankings. The one longitudinal classroom experiment (Hanus & Fox 2015) shows these lower intrinsic motivation AND exam scores over a semester. DRIP's 'flex' checkpoints compare the reader to an imagined public ('most engineers'), never to other users — keep it that way.
- No decorative juice. Animation, visuals, or 'fun fact' asides that don't carry the concept are seductive details (g = -0.33 on learning). Motion must reveal meaning — a payoff springing open, a diagram edge showing causal direction — never ambient sparkle. Every VisualSpec should have to justify itself semantically.
- No daily streak with pure loss framing, and never guilt copy ('don't lose your 12 days!'). Broken streaks suppress engagement below baseline when self-attributed; if any streak-like mechanic ever ships, repair mechanics ship the same day and breaks get externalized in persona voice. Safer: within-session hot-streak counters (consecutive correct bets) that reset without loss language.
- No autoplay or auto-advance, ever. It deletes the operant gesture (the entire swipe-as-lever mechanism), removes learner pacing (a named moderator of the segmenting benefit), and erases the reader's stopping decision — which is also the ethical line separating DRIP from the machine zone.
- Never fake the bottom of the feed. Do not soften or defer the crossroads stop to stretch sessions — TikTok's screen-time tools moved usage from 108.5 to 107 minutes and were internally described as trust theater. DRIP's stop-and-ask at topic boundaries is a real stop; that it asks a question is what makes it not a dead end.
- Don't reward skimming: a fast swipe past an interactive card must not advance progress framing or count toward checkpoint flexes — rewarding throughput over engagement trains exactly the shallow-skim behavior the seductive-details and multitasking literatures warn about.
- Don't copy the engagement playbook's interruption tactics — no mid-run pings, modals, or spliced-in system cards above the thumb (2.8-second interruptions double error rates on sequential tasks). The placeholder-under-the-thumb rule already protects identity; extend the same sanctity to attention.

## Displaying information on a phone card — editorial + data-viz craft for one-screen-at-a-ti

applied:
- Codify a 3-role card type scale as theme CSS variables (--fs-display / --fs-body / --fs-label): display is length-adaptive via the existing helpers.ts step tables, body 17–19px with 1.35–1.45 line-height, label 11–12px uppercase eyebrow. Lint card components so no fourth ad-hoc size can appear; hierarchy jumps must be large (display ≥ 1.8× body).
- Convert body max-width from px to ch: replace maxWidth: 340 with maxWidth: '36ch' (target 30–40 CPL) on every prose block so the characters-per-line invariant survives any font-size or font-family change the planner makes.
- Add a build/test-time contrast gate in lib/theme/cssVars.ts: planner-emitted hexes must satisfy ink-on-bg ≥ 7:1, ink-2-on-bg ≥ 4.5:1, accent-ink-on-accent ≥ 4.5:1; auto-nudge lightness toward compliance instead of rejecting the theme. Also forbid pure #000/#FFF ink by clamping toward the theme hue (the Wikipedia #252525 move, generalized per-theme).
- Never render a legend anywhere: DiagramCard and spark/stat visuals direct-label every node and endpoint; delete axis/grid furniture whenever the value is printed on the mark. On a 393px card the chart has one point — label that point, erase everything else.
- Make correct/wrong states triple-encoded: --state-correct/--state-wrong color + a check/x glyph + reveal copy that states the outcome in words. Add a Playwright greyscale screenshot pass (CSS filter: grayscale(1)) to the phase gate: if a card's hierarchy or state is unreadable in greyscale, it fails review.
- Write the tap-payoff rule into the writer prompt and variety enforcement: nothing pedagogically essential may live only behind a tap (glosses, tapNotes, reveals are bonus depth); and every reveal payoff must CHANGE the reader's model, not restate the setup — Tse's 'something spectacular has to happen' as a hard content rule.
- Split each card into an austere data zone and a flavored frame: signatureKind decoration, texture, and eyebrow stamps live at the card edges (hook/checkpoint headline zones) where Bateman/Borkin-style related embellishment buys recall; the interior of code/diagram/stat/spark zones is pure data-ink — no texture, no signature, one accent.
- Apply the BAN discipline to StatCard and the stat VisualSpec: a huge number only when the number is the message, always paired with exactly one comparison anchor in the label ('3ms — 100× faster than disk'); render the unit at ~30% of digit size and visually subordinate; prompt the writer to reject stats that need a trend line instead.
- Adopt a single 4/8px spacing scale as CSS variables consumed by CardFrame and all card bodies; audit current paddings to snap to it, and increase default whitespace one notch (Refactoring UI: start with too much, remove) — this is the cheapest single move toward premium feel.
- Formalize the icon policy in Visual.tsx: icons render large as accent-colored anchors above/beside a headline that carries the meaning; never inline as word substitutes, never as the only differentiator between two options on binary/predict cards.
- Use the working-memory finding to justify and tune context-carrying surfaces: eyebrow as a running thread breadcrumb, auto-gloss any term last seen >6 cards ago (storyline.covered already knows), and keep the recap cadence — pitch these in the brief as comprehension mechanics for small screens, not decoration.
- Give the display font a stage: hook cards should push headline size up via the length-adaptive steps until it nearly fills the width (Duolingo/BAN glanceability), with tighter line-height (~1.05–1.15) and per-theme tracking; body cards keep display type only in the headline role so the theme's display font stays a brand moment, not wallpaper.

refused (the anti-patterns):
- Do not import engagement-carousel tricks that inflate swipes without teaching — cliffhanger 'swipe for more' arrows, withholding the point until slide 8, curiosity-gap headlines that the next card doesn't pay off. Carousel engagement stats measure taps, not learning; DRIP's unit of value is understanding-per-card.
- Do not put load-bearing content behind taps or expanders. NN/g: accordions raise interaction cost; Tse: assume no one ever sees a tooltip. If removing every tap target from a card leaves it not teaching, the card is broken.
- Do not add chart furniture (legends, axes, gridlines, tick marks) to make a card visual look more 'serious' or chart-like — on one phone screen with one point, furniture is pure data-ink debt.
- Do not let type sizes proliferate. The template-y infographic look is five sizes + centered-everything + evenly-bold text; premium is three roles with big jumps and de-emphasized secondaries.
- Do not use pure #000 on #FFF, pure #FFF on saturated accents, or grey text on colored backgrounds — tint the secondary ink toward the background hue instead (Refactoring UI / Wikipedia #252525 pattern). This is exactly where AI-generated hex themes will silently fail, hence the contrast gate.
- Do not let signature decoration or texture cross into the data zone — related embellishment at the frame helps recall, but decoration overlapping numbers, code, or diagram nodes is the distraction case the memorability literature actually warns about.
- Do not encode correct/wrong (or any state) in color alone — ~8% of male readers have CVD and WCAG 1.4.1 forbids it; every state needs a glyph or word alongside the color.
- Do not copy Duolingo's surface aesthetic (super-saturated palette, mascot juice, rounded-everything). Copy its invariants — consistent chrome placement, one task per screen, 3-minute economy — but DRIP's whole theming premise is that each subject gets its own editorial identity; a fixed candy skin would kill the differentiator.

## Learning science for micro-content — what makes 15-second units actually teach (evidence a

applied:
- Add a confidence tap to scored cards (binary/predict/sequence): after picking, one more tap — 'lock it in' vs 'hunch' (or press-and-hold to stake). High-confidence misses get a visually louder, surprise-framed reveal (hypercorrection), a guaranteed re-ask at NEAR_GAP, AND a cross-session due-date. Confidence also de-noises the ability estimate that lib/adapt/anchors.ts currently patches with a flat guessRate.
- Make topic entry a pretest: the first card of each outline node becomes a bet the reader cannot yet know, with a writer contract that the following 2–3 cards MUST directly answer the prequestioned fact (the g=0.66 effect is specific; unanswered hooks earn nothing). The reveal copy should reference the reader's actual guess ('you said X — it's Y, and here's the gap').
- New tiny card type 'reason' (menu-based self-explanation): after a reveal or concept, 'call the reason' — 2–3 candidate mechanisms, one correct, ≤1 per batch, disguised as a hot-take poll. This imports a g=0.55 intervention DRIP has zero coverage of today.
- Cross-session spaced callbacks: persist due anchors with real timestamps (day-1 and week-1 tiers per Cepeda's ridgeline). When the reader returns, open the feed with 3–5 'still got it?' bets from previous sessions before new content — this doubles as the resume-first home screen the owner wants for session usability.
- Re-ask, never re-tell, after corrections: for any missed bet (especially high-confidence misses), the scheduled callback must be another retrieval attempt on the same anchor, because corrected errors return within a week without an intervening test.
- Discrimination-pair interleaving: when two anchors within a topic are confusable (shared stem words in lib/adapt/anchors.ts is a ready heuristic), schedule a 'which one is this?' binary or compare-variant diagram that juxtaposes them. This is where interleaving's g=0.42 actually lives; keep the existing one-out-of-node cap for everything else.
- Calibrated flexing at checkpoints: keep flex copy but ground it in the retrieval record — 'you called 7 of 9 bets on X' — and show the confidence-vs-outcome gap when it's flattering-adjacent ('your hunches hit 80%'). This punctures fluency-driven illusion-of-knowing without a single banned word, and it is the honest version of the dopamine loop.
- Integration cards on the storyline: every ~15–20 cards, a planner-driven 'the map so far' diagram (layers/flow variant) placing the last 3–4 anchors in one structure. Recap is miss-triggered; this is schema-building, the thing micro-content demonstrably lacks. It also gives the timeline UI a natural 'chapter' anatomy without school vocabulary.
- Causal threading writer contract: within a thread, each card's opening line must connect causally to the prior card (because/so/but), and the storyline spine should be written as a causal arc (tension → turn → payoff), not a topic list — the narrative advantage rides on causal connectivity, and it is also what makes the next scroll feel owed.
- Coherence lint on generation: reject or rewrite cards whose visual/stat/aside does not serve the current anchor (seductive-details penalty g≈−0.4). The variety governor's 'every batch carries a visual' quota must never be satisfied by a decorative stat — the visual must depict the mechanism.
- Turn session setup into errorful generation: where a clarify card fits, prefer 'before we start — what do you think X actually does?' guesses over preference questions. It starts the pretest loop at second zero, gives the planner a real prior-knowledge signal, and makes creating a session feel like the game has already begun.
- Feed confidence into difficulty adaptation: high-confidence hits escalate difficulty faster than dwell alone; low-confidence hits (lucky/unsure) hold level. The ~80% hit-rate target is defensible — keep it — but it should be a target on confident hits, not raw taps.

refused (the anti-patterns):
- Autoplay or timed auto-advance between cards. The segmenting effect's benefit is contingent on learner-controlled pacing; system-paced delivery is where multimedia design failures concentrate. The thumb must always own the scroll.
- Seductive-detail dopamine: meme cards, 'crazy fact' asides, decorative animations unrelated to the current anchor. These reliably raise watch time and reliably LOWER learning (g ≈ −0.37 to −0.41). Delight must come from the mechanism being shown, not from garnish.
- Optimizing for felt-smoothness or self-reported satisfaction. Fluent delivery inflates perceived learning with zero effect on actual learning — if the north-star metric is 'feels great' or dwell time, DRIP will maximize illusion-of-knowing. The learning metric must be callback hit-rate.
- Streaks and rewards keyed to consumption volume (cards seen, minutes scrolled, days opened). That rewards passive exposure — the one demonstrated failure mode of feed learning. Any variable-reward loop must key on retrieval outcomes: bets called, callbacks survived, confidence calibration.
- Same-frame instant gratification on questions — confetti and the answer in the same tap. The next-slide reveal is simultaneously the correct feedback design (guaranteed processing of the correction) and the better dopamine loop (a curiosity gap the scroll closes). Do not collapse it.
- Novelty-hopping disguised as interleaving. Jumping topics every card maximizes freshness and destroys schema; interleaving only pays between confusable, related concepts. Keep the storyline dominant and the out-of-node callback cap tight.
- Chasing a seconds-per-card KPI. Nothing in the evidence blesses 15 seconds; it blesses one idea per learner-paced unit. Splitting one idea across three under-filled cards to hit a duration target is fragmentation — the exact charge against TikTok learning.
- Letting adaptive compression strip all retrieval. When the pace directive says 'compress', suppressing callbacks is right — but a long fully-passive run recreates the passive-watching failure mode. Compression should shrink prose, not zero out interaction indefinitely.

## Scroll/swipe mechanics and progress indication in vertical snap feeds (iOS Safari PWA focu

applied:
- Keep the native CSS snap scroller (y mandatory + snap-stop always) — iOS's snap-on-release bug makes one-card-per-flick the native feel anyway, and it inherits UIScrollView-grade physics for free. Do not rebuild as a JS transform feed.
- After every batch insertion, placeholder swap, or runway deletion, force a synchronous reflow (read `containerRef.current.offsetHeight`) to bust WebKit's cached snap points — this directly protects the 'placeholder under the thumb is sacred' guarantee on iOS.
- Add settle detection: use `scrollend` where supported (Safari 26.2+) with a ~150ms debounced scroll-timer fallback; queue all DOM mutations (batch merges, card insertion below frontier) to fire only at settle, never mid-gesture.
- Timeline model redesign, same grammar sharper physics: keep horizontal segments but make topic boundaries real gaps (YouTube chapters), make segment width proportional to planned topic size, widen the active segment slightly (focus+context), render a fork as a visible notch with everything downstream dimmed — structure readable at a glance, zero numbers.
- Animate the buffered 'ghost' band with a slow backward-drifting shimmer while the writer is running (Harrison: backward decelerating motion = 11% faster perceived), and ramp the nib's pulse frequency as the batch nears ready (increasing frequency preferred over constant); freeze all animation the moment generation stops so the bar never lies.
- Exploit endowed progress: when a topic opens, snap its segment to ~10–15% filled the moment the hook card is viewed (raise MIN_READ from 0.03 and animate the grant) — the head start must be visible to work (34% vs 19% completion in the field study).
- Exploit goal gradient: past ~75% of a segment, add a subtle brighten/swell on the remaining sliver — visible nearness to the topic boundary measurably accelerates completion; pair the boundary crossing with the checkpoint card's flex copy.
- Add a Kindle-style on-demand effort estimate: single tap on the timeline shows '~2 min left in this thread' computed from the session's own median dwell (no percentages, no counts — time is effort, not grade); long-press keeps opening the full session map.
- Spend the vertical/map metaphor in SessionMapSheet, not the ambient bar: render the outline as a Duolingo-style vertical path — done nodes solid, buffered nodes ghosted, planned nodes as outline dots, forks as literal branches — so 'what exists ahead' has one canonical, glanceable answer.
- Style the crossroads stop as a 'caught up' achievement moment (Instagram precedent): the segment it ends visibly completes, the bar rests, and the choice card reads as a rest point earned — and structurally guarantee nothing auto-generates past it.
- At the end of buffered runway, don't let the feed hard-stop: give the last card an overscroll stretch (translate a fraction of drag distance, ~0.15 compliance like Swiper's resistanceRatio 0.85 inverse, spring back at release) so 'nothing yet' feels like elastic resistance, not a wall — the themed notice card remains the only waiting surface.
- If a JS gesture layer is ever added (peek-next-card, pull-to-refresh on the hairline), commit on distance>50% OR flick<300ms (Swiper defaults), spring stiffness 300–600/damping 30–50 with gesture velocity as the spring's initial velocity, ~5px dead zone — and keep it additive on top of native scroll, never replacing it.

refused (the anti-patterns):
- Do not copy TikTok's zero-progress-indication. It works for disposable entertainment; for 'actually useful learning' it recreates NN/g's documented failures — no effort calibration, no sense of position, illusion of completeness. DRIP's differentiator is that the feed has a spine you can see.
- Do not copy TikTok web's JS-transform/virtualized scroller. You forfeit native momentum, rubber-banding, accessibility, and UIScrollView physics, and inherit every touch edge case; the native snap container plus small fixes (reflow after mutation) is strictly better on iOS.
- Do not use `scroll-snap-type: proximity` or drop `snap-stop: always` to chase a 'flingy' feel — iOS snaps on release regardless (WebKit 243582), proximity is the buggier path in WebKit, and letting flings skip cards breaks one-thought-per-screen comprehension.
- Do not render numeric progress anywhere on the bar or map — no counts, no percentages, no 'x of y segments'. This is both the Prime Directive and the evidence: the value of stories/chapters grammar is that structure reads without arithmetic; numbers turn flexing into grading.
- Do not fake progress motion: no bar movement while generation is stalled, no nonlinear inflation, and never let the ghost band claim runway that a re-plan deleted. Harrison 2007 found pauses and end-stage stalls are perceived worst of all — a bar caught lying once is dead.
- Do not put auto-generated 'suggested' content after the crossroads stop the way Instagram appended Suggested Posts after 'You're All Caught Up' — the moment the stop stops being honest, the entire timeline loses its meaning as a truth surface.
- Do not make the timeline permanently full-opacity chrome. Stories bars run hot because a story lasts 5 seconds; over a minutes-long session, persistent chrome reads as a course header. Ambient (bright on movement, fade at rest, on-demand depth) is the feed-native register.
- Do not make audio or haptics information-bearing. iOS Safari has no vibrate API and Web Audio dies with the ringer switch, so any tick is heard by a minority — every state change must be fully legible from the visual spring alone.

## The cold start — session creation UX for a paste-anything learning app (input affordances,

applied:
- Add a paste chip to NewSessionSheet: when the sheet opens with an empty textarea, show one chip — 'paste what you copied' — that reads the clipboard ONLY on tap (explicit user gesture, no iOS permission scare), runs it through the existing routeInput(), and labels what it found ('looks like a link', 'looks like an article'). Never read the clipboard on open.
- Unfurl URLs inside the sheet before submit: when routeInput detects a URL, immediately kick the ingest call and render a small unfurl row (favicon-less domain, page title from ingest, rough length: 'a 12-minute read') above the textarea. This converts the current opaque busy='reading' state into visible labor, catches dead links before the user commits, and lets them correct the paste without losing the sheet.
- Replace the placeholder-only empty state with suggested starts that teach by example: 3 rotating tappable chips under 'paste anything. scroll it in.' — one curiosity sentence ('why do planes actually stay up'), one paste-shaped ('paste a paper you're pretending to have read'), one URL-shaped. Tap pre-fills the sheet textarea (never auto-submits). Same 3 chips appear in the sheet when the textarea is empty. Rotate from a hand-written pool; they are the app's first writing sample.
- Kill the blocking 'brewing' wait: POST /api/sessions should return immediately and navigation to /s/{id} should happen at once; the feed's existing planning poll becomes a narrated planning sequence — a full-screen themed stage card cycling REAL pipeline stages ('reading your text' → 'naming this world' → 'pouring the first cards'), each tied to actual backend progress events, per the labor illusion. The theme arriving mid-narration (colors flooding in) is itself a delight beat.
- Set a hard latency budget and instrument it: tap-on-'drip it' → first real card < 10s (Nielsen attention limit), first visible response < 1s (sheet dismiss + navigation), narration covering every interval > 1s. Log the distribution; the plan model call is the long pole, so consider having the planner emit firstCards streamed/early so a validated hook card can render before the outline finishes.
- If any skeleton is shown for the incoming first card, make it the true hook-card layout (eyebrow bar, huge headline block) with a slow left-to-right shimmer, swapped for the real card the instant it validates — and cap it at a few seconds before falling back to the narrated stage card. Never generic gray boxes for a 5–10s AI wait (Viget failure mode).
- Persist sheet drafts: mirror the textarea to localStorage on change and restore on next open, with a tiny 'picked up where you left off' note. Losing a pasted wall of text to an accidental sheet dismiss is a cold-start killer; it also makes the busy-state error path ('that didn't go through') recoverable for free.
- Endow progress at birth: when planning completes, the session's progress ring/hairline should already sit visibly non-zero (the plan + first cards count as distance traveled), and the first checkpoint's flex copy should acknowledge it. Per Nunes & Drèze this measurably raises completion — and it's honest: work genuinely happened.
- Turn SessionTile into a resume card: add one teaser line pulled from the next unviewed card ('next: the footgun') plus soft recency ('2 days ago'), and sort the grid by resume intent — mid-thread sessions (abandoned between checkpoints, recently active) rank above finished or barely-started ones — Netflix's continue-watching signals mapped to feed position. Tap still lands exactly on the reader's frontier card.
- Make creation background-safe (Suno pattern): a just-created session appears in the home grid instantly with its live stage label replacing the current static 'brewing…' — so a user who backgrounds the PWA mid-planning returns to a tile that is visibly further along, and tapping it always lands somewhere alive (narration or first cards), never a dead spinner.
- Apply Harrison pacing to any determinate-feeling planning indicator: progress motion should accelerate toward the end (back-load the fast part) and use slow decelerating texture motion, since the real pipeline front-loads its longest step (plan model call) — a linear bar would crawl exactly when attention is most fragile.
- Keep the option surface flat: chill + depth stay as one-glance toggles inside the sheet, never a pre-creation step or wizard; anything the planner genuinely needs to ask becomes an in-feed clarify card AFTER first cards exist (Duolingo's value-before-commitment result applied to configuration instead of signup).

refused (the anti-patterns):
- Auto-reading the clipboard when the sheet or app opens. On iOS this fires the 'Allow Paste?' modal / paste banner and reads as spying (TikTok-pasteboard-scandal energy). Clipboard access must live inside an explicit tap on a paste affordance — the tap is the consent.
- Fake narrated stages. The labor illusion only pays when transparency is honest; stage copy that loops, lies about what's happening, or finishes long before/after the real work teaches users the narration is theater, and the trust never comes back. Bind every stage line to a real pipeline event.
- Deliberately padding latency because 'users rated 9s as more thoughtful'. That finding is about perception of unavoidable waits, not license to slow down; DRIP's directive is invisible waiting via buffering. Use real work time, narrate it, and race to shrink it.
- Tappable suggestion chips in the thumb's scroll path, or suggestion taps that do anything destructive/irreversible (Perplexity's mobile bug: brushing a suggested question could wipe the conversation). Suggested starts must pre-fill, never submit, never replace.
- Filling the empty home with engagement bait — trending topics, other people's feeds, streak banners, 'your feed misses you' guilt notifications as resume hooks. The shelf is the user's own artifacts; the resume pull should be content-forward (the next hook's tease), not FOMO mechanics.
- Numeric progress anywhere on session tiles or planning ('12/47 cards', '80% planned'). Violates the Prime Directive's no-grading rule; the ring, the hairline, and flex copy carry state without numbers.
- A pre-creation onboarding wizard (goal? audience? level? — three screens before the textarea). Every screen before first value bleeds activation; Duolingo's data says let them taste it first. DRIP already asks in-feed via clarify cards — keep it that way.
- Generic full-screen skeletons as the default AI wait. Viget's data shows unfamiliar skeletons perform worse than a plain spinner for perceived speed; a long shimmering wireframe over a 10s generation is the exact condition where they fail. Skeletons only for sub-few-second, layout-faithful swaps.
